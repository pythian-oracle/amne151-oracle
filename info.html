AMNE 151 Project Proposal: key elements

1,000–1,500 words plus bibliography. Groups add 500 words for each additional person.

Due Fri 23 Oct by 10 p.m. on Canvas. Add to calendar: Google · Apple/Outlook

Please email me (tesa@student.ubc.ca) or use the oracle to send an idea for approval.

To give yourself time to finish writing, send your idea by Tue 20 Oct, 10 p.m. Late ideas will still get comments, but lateness will affect your mark. Add to calendar: Google · Apple/Outlook

1. The idea

Write up the idea: what you plan to make, which myth it's about, and how it inspires you.
Examples: play, poem, hymn, story, painting, animation, fresco, vase, graphic novel, song lyrics, podcast with ancient guest, votive plaque, stage set design, escape room, engraved coin, statue, mechanism, video, architectural sketch or model, textile, board game, video game, map, initiation ritual, scientific illustration, data visualization, etc.

2. Primary sources

Name the specific texts or material culture you're using, and the specific passages or parts within them. Explain how each one inspired what you're trying to do. Cite them correctly in Chicago style.

3. Secondary sources

Survey the field for works similar to your proposal. For example, if your concept is a video game, discuss what other video games have been published, what their approaches were, and how yours will differ.

In addition, identify themes or approaches you wish to engage with, and survey the scholarly literature for relevant work. For example, if part of your plan is to foreground the experience of children, you can discuss what we know or don't know about children in ancient Greece and Rome.

4. Form

What will your project deliverable look like? You can propose a large project with a plan to complete a section, for example one scene in a movie. Be as detailed as possible. If it's a video, how long will it be? How many actors and scenes? If it's an artwork, what medium will you use and what size canvas or paper?

5. Creating in 2026

Discuss what it means to create something today from inspiration rooted in the ancient world. What are your artistic goals in reinterpreting or reinventing myth? What challenges do you anticipate?

Bibliography

List your primary and secondary sources, cited correctly in Chicago style.

Bibliography Citation Format

Chicago Manual of Style 16th or 17th edition (a.k.a. Turabian for undergraduate work)
Font: Times New Roman, 12 point
Note: "Chicago Humanities" and "Chicago Author-Date" only differ when it comes to footnotes/endnotes. Otherwise, the bibliography part is the same for both.

Step by Step for Each Type

  1. Primary Source
    1. go to Loeb Classical
    2. find a book relevant to your topic
    3. citation is at the bottom of Table of Contents page
    4. it's already perfectly formatted for Chicago Humanities; put book title in italics.
    5. add an annotation: use complete sentences.

✔️Pindar. Olympian Odes. Pythian Odes. Edited and translated by William H. Race. Loeb Classical Library 56. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1997.

This book has the original Greek and the English translation on facing pages. The very helpful introduction includes background context and stylistic notes.

  1. Book
    1. Go to a database
      1. UBC Library Catalog: https://ubc.summon.serialssolutions.com/search
      2. Oxford Bibliographies Online: https://www.oxfordbibliographies.com/
      3. Annee Philologique: https://resources.library.ubc.ca/page.php?id=2101
      4. https://scholar.google.com
    2. It's a book if it has an ISBN or it's in the UBC Catalog as a book or eBook
    3. Clean up the citation by hand
      1. multiple publication locations: pick one, ideally the main headquarters.
      2. fix capitalization: the title should be capitalized in Chicago's "headline style."

❌Braswell, Bruce Karl. A Commentary on the Fourth Pythian Ode of Pindar. Vol. Bd. 14.;Bd. 14;. New York;Berlin;: De Gruyter, 1988.

✔️Braswell, Bruce Karl. A Commentary on the Fourth Pythian Ode of Pindar. Vol. 14. Berlin: De Gruyter, 1988.

  1. Book Section
    1. find a book "edited by" and select the chapter relevant to your topic
    2. sometimes the ebook has "cite" link for that chapter, sometimes not.
    3. check UBC library catalog, Google scholar for a citation to start with.
    4. assemble the citation by hand; put book name in italics.

❌Smith, Roland RR. "Pindar, athletes, and the early Greek statue habit." Pindar’s Poetry, Patrons, and Festivals: From Archaic Greece to the Roman Empire (2007): 83-139. (from Google Scholar)

❌Hornblower, Simon and Catherine Morgan. Pindar's Poetry, Patrons, and Festivals: From Archaic Greece to the Roman Empire, edited by Hornblower, Simon, Catherine Morgan. Oxford;Toronto;: Oxford University Press, 2007. (from UBC Catalog)

✔️Smith, R. R. R. “Pindar, Athletes, and the Early Greek Statue Habit.” In Pindar’s Poetry, Patrons, and Festivals : from Archaic Greece to the Roman Empire, edited by Simon Hornblower and Catherine Morgan, 83–139. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2007.

  1. Journal Article
    1. find a journal article relevant to your topic:
      e.g. Classical Reception Journal: https://academic.oup.com/crj/issue
    2. fix citation by hand: fix capitals to be headline style; put journal name in italics..

❌Potamiti, Anna. "the Theme of Hospitality in Pindar's Fourth Pythian." Greece and Rome 62, no. 1 (2015): 1-11. Doi: 10.1017/S0017383514000205.

✔️Potamiti, Anna. “The Theme of Hospitality in Pindar’s Fourth Pythian.” Greece and Rome 62, no. 1 (2015): 1-11. Doi: 10.1017/S0017383514000205.

Note: if there's a DOI, great. If not, fine too. Do not let your bibliography software include the long ugly UBC library URL like this: http://ubc.summon.serialssolutions.com/2.0.0/link/0/eLvHCXMwfV07D8Ig...

  1. Primary visual/material culture source
    1. Find an object relevant to your topic
      1. British Museum
      2. Louvre Museum
      3. Perseus Catalog https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/artifactBrowser
    2. format it by hand; there's no standard description for each item; just make it concise.
      Description. Object Type. Collection. Identifier. URL.
