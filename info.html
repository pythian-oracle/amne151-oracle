AMNE 151 Project Proposal: key elements

1,000–1,500 words plus bibliography. Groups add 500 words for each additional person.

Due Fri 23 Oct by 10 p.m. on Canvas.

Use the oracle to send an idea for approval.

To give yourself time to finish writing, send your idea by Tue 20 Oct, 10 p.m. at the very latest.

1. The idea

Write up the idea: what you plan to make, which myth it's about, and how it inspires you.
Examples: play, poem, hymn, story, painting, animation, fresco, vase, graphic novel, song lyrics, podcast with ancient guest, votive plaque, stage set design, escape room, engraved coin, statue, mechanism, video, architectural sketch or model, textile, board game, video game, map, initiation ritual, scientific illustration, data visualization, etc.

You can work with any primary source in Greek mythology, not just the ones in assigned reading.

2. Primary sources

For your mythological figure or narrative, find a variety of primary sources and discuss how they differ and why you might favor one over another. Name the specific texts or material culture you will be using, and the specific passages or parts within them. Explain how each one inspired what you're trying to do. What concept or perspective do you want to bring out? Cite your sources correctly in Chicago style.

3. Secondary sources

Survey the field for works similar to your proposal. For example, if your concept is a video game, discuss what other video games have been published, what their approaches were, and how yours will differ.

In addition, identify themes or approaches you wish to engage with, and survey the scholarly literature for relevant work. For example, if part of your plan is to foreground the experience of children, you can discuss what we know or don't know about children in ancient Greece and Rome.

4. Form

What will your project deliverable look like? You can propose a large project with a plan to complete a section, for example one scene in a movie. Be as detailed as possible. If it's a video, how long will it be? How many actors and scenes? If it's an artwork, what medium will you use and what size canvas or paper?

5. Creating with Intention

What will you add to the conversation? What are your artistic goals in reinterpreting or reinventing myth? What challenges do you anticipate?

Bibliography

Cite your sources in footnotes in Chicago notes-bibliography style (the same style the oracle uses when you make an offering). At the end of your document, list all the sources you used in alphabetical order by author, in Chicago bibliography style.

Bibliography Guide

Grading

The proposal is graded on writing mechanics, the depth of thinking in your concept, and how well you engage with correctly cited primary sources.

Write formally, as you might in an application for a scholarship or grant from an arts organization. Proofread for correct spelling and grammar.

Include at least three sources. For example, if your artwork draws on a single primary source, your proposal can consider different translations, similar or contrasting narratives in Greek or other mythology, as well as secondary sources. This is a good place to show off your research skills!
